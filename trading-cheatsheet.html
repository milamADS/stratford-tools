🕯️Candlestick Patterns

Each candle = one time period. Green close > open (buyers won). Red close < open (sellers won). Long body = strong pressure · short body = indecision · long wick = rejection at that level. Reversal patterns are strongest after a trend and when the next candle confirms.

Reality check: patterns lose potency within ~3–5 candles and fail often. One clue only — confirm with trend, support/resistance, and volume.

🎯When to Enter

Good entries come from confluence — several signals agreeing — not one magic trigger.

Support & Resistance (the foundation)

  • Support = a "floor" where falling prices tend to stop (buyers step in).
  • Resistance = a "ceiling" where rising prices stall (sellers step in).
  • Longer time frame = more significant level. At a level, price either bounces or breaks — both tradable.

The 4 Entry Approaches

1 · Bounce off support/resistance ▶

Buy near tested support (expect a bounce), sell/short near resistance. Advantage: place a tight stop just beyond the level, so being wrong costs little.

2 · Breakout ▶

Enter when price decisively breaks resistance (up) or support (down), ideally on rising volume. High volume = "real"; low-volume breakouts often fail ("fakeouts").

3 · Pullback / retest ▶

After a breakout, price often returns to retest the broken level — old resistance becomes new support. Entering the pullback = better price + tighter stop than chasing.

4 · Trend confirmation ▶

"The trend is your friend." In an uptrend, buy pullbacks; in a downtrend, sell rallies. Fighting a strong trend is a classic beginner mistake.

📊Indicators (Plain English)

Indicators are derived from price — they lag and confirm; they don't predict. Use together, never alone.

IndicatorWhat it tells youKey signal
Moving Avg (MA)Average price over N periods (9/20/50/100/200). Acts as dynamic support/resistance.Price above rising MA = uptrend bias. Golden cross (short crosses above long) bullish; death cross bearish.
RSIMomentum 0–100. Above 70 overbought, below 30 oversold.Some buy on cross back up through 30, sell on cross down through 70. Warning: in strong trends it can stay stretched for a long time.
MACDMomentum & trend from two MAs + a signal line.MACD above signal = bullish momentum; below = bearish. Divergence from price warns of weakening.
VolumeThe "conviction meter" — shares/contracts traded.Moves on high volume are more trustworthy. Rising price on falling volume = running out of fuel.

💡 Confluence example

Price pulls back to a rising 50-day MA that lines up with prior support · RSI turning up off oversold · a bullish engulfing candle forms on above-average volume. Several signals agreeing = higher-quality setup than any one alone.

⏰Best Time to Trade

Stocks (U.S. 9:30–16:00 ET)

WindowCharacter
Open 9:30–10:30Busiest, most volatile — most opportunity and risk. Many beginners avoid the first few minutes.
Midday 11:30–14:00Calmest, lowest volume, "drifty." Tight ranges, fewer clean setups, worse fills.
Close 15:00–16:00Surge as institutions/day traders position for end of day. Active but risky.

Volume & volatility concentrate at open/close = liquidity = tighter spreads. The dead middle can hurt you with poor fills.

Crypto (24/7/365)

  • Never closes — opportunity anytime, but no break; big moves happen while you sleep.
  • Liquidity still varies: deeper during US morning / European afternoon overlap, thin late night & weekends.
  • Low-liquidity windows = sharp "wicky" moves. Stops & alerts matter even more.

General principles

  • Avoid low-liquidity times (wider spreads, slippage).
  • Trade an asset during its "prime time."
  • Be cautious around major news (earnings, Fed, data releases) — spreads widen, beginners get chopped up.
  • You can't reliably time exact tops/bottoms. Don't build a strategy on calendar superstition.

🛡️Risk Management Most important

Great entries with bad risk management still blow up accounts. "Plan the trade, and trade the plan."

Stop Losses

  • Pre-decided exit price for a losing trade — set before you enter.
  • Defeats the "it'll come back" mentality — the #1 beginner account-killer.
  • Place just beyond a logical level (below support for a long, above resistance for a short). Give it room for noise, not so much the loss is huge.
  • Also plan a take-profit target (often the next S/R).

Position Sizing — the 1% Rule

Never risk more than 1% of your account per trade (2% max if you can afford it). On a $10,000 account that's $100 of risk — the most you'd lose if stopped out, not the position size.

Sizing: dollar risk ÷ per-share risk = shares. e.g. $100 ÷ ($50 entry − $48 stop = $2) = 50 shares. → Try the calculator tab

Risk / Reward

  • Risking $100 to make $300 = 1:3.
  • Good R:R means you can be wrong more often than right and still profit — at 1:2 you only need ~34% win rate to break even.
  • Many won't take a setup below 1:2 (some insist on 1:3).

Habits that keep you alive

  • Pre-decide entry, stop & target — no improvising once emotions kick in.
  • Don't over-leverage — it magnifies losses and can wipe you in one move.
  • Don't revenge-trade — chasing losses with bigger bets kills accounts.
  • Keep a trading journal — log setup, size, stop, result & why; review it.
  • Use a demo/paper account first. Accept losses as part of the game.

✅Beginner Pre-Trade Checklist

Run through this before every trade. Click to check off.

  1. Context — what's the overall trend? Am I trading with it or against it?
  2. Level — is price at meaningful support/resistance?
  3. Signal — do candlestick + indicator(s) + volume agree (confluence)?
  4. Risk first — where's my stop? Position size at 1%? R:R at least ~1:2?
  5. Timing — is this a reasonably liquid time to trade this asset?
  6. Plan the exit — both stop and target set before clicking buy.
  7. Journal it — then review honestly.
Final word: technical analysis is a framework, not a crystal ball. The biggest edge a beginner can build is discipline in risk management — small size, defined stops, favorable R:R, no emotional trading. Learn slowly, keep positions tiny, expect to lose while learning.
